Why It Stays Open

Federal benefits are their own language.

A federal employee's retirement isn't one plan — it's five interlocking systems with their own rules, deadlines, and irreversible elections. Most agents never learn them, so most feds never get advice.

  • FERS — high-3 math, service computation dates, MRA rules, and the supplement.
  • TSP — fund selection, catch-up contributions, and what to do at separation.
  • FEGLI — Basic and Options A/B/C, and premiums that climb steeply in retirement.
  • FEHB — the 5-year rule, Medicare coordination, and the survivor advantage.
  • Survivor benefits — elections that are permanent the day they're filed.

Get one of those wrong in front of a 30-year GS-13 and the meeting is over. Get them right and you're the person they send their whole office to.

They can't get answers internally.

HR can process paperwork. It can't tell someone whether to keep FEGLI Option B, when to retire, or what to do with a $600K TSP balance.

They don't trust cold outreach.

Federal employees are trained to be skeptical of solicitation. A credible platform and a real analysis get you in the door; a cold dial does not.

The decisions are time-boxed.

Open season, MRA windows, the 5-year rule, separation dates. There is always a clock — which is exactly what makes a scheduled review land.
